# Introduction

Public data on firm hiring became machine-readable only recently, and what happens to a workforce when a general-purpose technology arrives has been hard to answer because the exposure measure and the employment record have never been observable in the same panel. That gap is what has kept the question open: credible variation in exposure has been difficult to come by. We assemble the two records for the first time.

We estimate the effect using a shift-share design in which pre-period occupation shares are interacted with a technology shock dated to a single quarter. Our instrument is the pre-period share, which is plausibly valid because it is fixed before the shock and is uncorrelated with contemporaneous demand once industry and year effects are absorbed. We describe the panel construction briefly and leave the details to Section 3.

We find a precisely estimated null: the point estimate is 0.0005 with a standard error of 0.0024, which rules out displacement larger than 1.2 percent at conventional levels. This implies that the adjustment margin is hiring rather than separation, which matters because policy aimed at separations would target a margin that is not moving. Our findings are robust to alternative exposure measures, two-way clustering, and dropping the largest sector.

We extend the analysis in two directions. We split by firm size and by pre-period leverage, and we test whether the effect concentrates in the tradable sector. We find that the null holds in every subsample, and the tradable split is where the confidence interval is tightest, which is the opposite of what a demand story would predict.

We build on work measuring technology exposure at the occupation level, and extend it by bringing the measure to the firm. By focusing on incumbent workforces, our study complements research on entry and new-firm hiring (Doe et al. 2012; Smith et al. 2013). We are the first to observe exposure and employment in the same panel.

# Review of Financial Studies (RFS): publication standards

What differs from [shared-norms.md](shared-norms.md). Source tags resolve in
[sources.md](sources.md); untagged judgment calls are marked
`[field norm, unsourced]`.

## a. Desk and novelty criteria

- Acceptance rates by SFS's own count: 6.0% (2023, 1,534 submissions), 6.0%
  (2024, 1,544), 8.1% (2025, 1,474), 7.27% (2026, 1,664) [S9].
- Editors "desk reject papers they think are very unlikely to meet the
  journal's publication criteria" to protect turnaround; desk rejection does
  not signal the work is bad, only that the editor judges it a poor fit [S8].
  The median 30-35 day first decision is achieved largely through fast desk
  decisions [S9, S8].
- RFS is historically the most methods-forward of the three: openness to new
  measures, text methods, and ML, conditional on validation and economic
  interpretation [field norm, unsourced].
- The editorial team aims to complete decisions "in two or three rounds" [S8],
  so a contribution must be deliverable within that horizon; papers needing
  open-ended iteration die [field norm, unsourced].

## b. Methods and identification norms

- New constructed measures need validation against an incumbent measure, and
  measurement-error discussion is expected for any constructed variable
  [field norm, unsourced].
- ML and text methods are admissible when the economic object is clear;
  a predictive exercise with no economic interpretation is the standing
  desk-rejection archetype for this journal [field norm, unsourced].
- Mechanism tests connecting the empirical result to the proposed economics
  are weighted heavily relative to pure reduced-form effect sizes
  [field norm, unsourced].

## c. Data norms

- Authors must confirm compliance with the SFS code sharing policy before
  finalizing submission; exemptions are requested through the submission
  system [S7].
- Working-paper posting is explicitly permitted: prior circulation in a
  working-paper series does not violate the prior-publication rule when it
  predates acceptance [per academic.oup.com/rfs Instructions to Authors;
  confidence MEDIUM, page partially retrieved].

## d. Writing and presentation norms

- Abstract over 100 words gets the submission returned without review [S7].
- Double-spaced manuscript; Word or LaTeX accepted [S7; OUP instructions].
- No stated page cap on the SFS submission page [S7]; length discipline
  follows the shared Cochrane norm (40 pages as the good-practice target)
  [S10].

## e. Submission mechanics

- Fees: high-income economies $400 (SFS members) / $460 (non-members);
  middle-income $260/$320; low-income waived. Appeals $860 [S7].
- Desk-rejection partial refund: $200 (high-income fee) or $130
  (middle-income fee); no referee report is provided on desk rejection [S8].
- 120-day guarantee: "If we take longer than 120 days to send your decision,
  we will refund your submission fee" [S8].
- Turnaround: median 30 days / mean 34 (2025-26); median 33 / mean 38
  (2024-25); median 35 / mean 41 (2022-23) [S9].
- R&R resubmission window: 18 months from the decision (effective November 1,
  2024); later resubmissions are treated as new submissions [S8].
- Dual submission with select conferences is permitted under stated
  conditions; invited papers submit free in the first round only [S7].
- Authors may state editor preferences, accommodated subject to workload and
  conflicts [S7].
- A formal appeal process exists for rejections the authors believe rest on
  referee error [S8].

## Observed norms from exemplars (Phase B)

From a structured reading of 30 recent articles (5 deep reads; two
deep reads are pre-acceptance working papers, see `selection_bias_summary`):

- Abstract: median 100 words; over the cap the submission is returned [S7].
- Median 3 authors; median 43 published pages.
- Introductions run long: median 9 working-paper pages, about 3,400 words,
  11 substantive paragraphs, reflecting the space RFS papers spend on
  measurement validation and identification defense inside the intro.
- The heaviest table load of the three: median 8 main tables, 5 figures;
  4 of 5 deep reads reference an internet/online appendix.
- Every deep-read intro anchors magnitudes with base rates attached
  (an 11.3pp effect quoted against a 66.1% base).
- Numbered hypotheses rare (1 of 5); roadmap paragraphs rare (1 of 5).
- Recurring intro spine: quantified market-level anchor or measurement gap,
  the neglected actor/measure named, "This paper..." by paragraph 2, empirical
  facts first, then model or validation, selection threats answered inside
  the intro.
- Archetypes in sample match the journal's reputation: a text-measurement
  paper whose contribution is the validated measure itself, and micro
  evidence + structural model combinations.

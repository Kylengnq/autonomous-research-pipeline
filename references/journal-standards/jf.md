# Journal of Finance (JF): publication standards

What differs from [shared-norms.md](shared-norms.md). Source tags resolve in
[sources.md](sources.md); untagged judgment calls are marked
`[field norm, unsourced]`.

## a. Desk and novelty criteria

- JF self-describes as publishing "leading research across all major fields of
  financial research" for an audience of over 8,000 academics, professionals,
  and institutions [S1]: the desk test is broad interest across finance, not
  depth within a subfield [field norm, unsourced].
- Desk rejection ran 43% in 2023 and the editor "foresee[s] this number
  stabilizing at around 45%"; the stated aim is to desk reject when the editors
  "cannot see a reasonable chance that a submitted paper could eventually clear
  the bar" [S3].
- Conditional on surviving the desk, the R&R probability was 16.5% in 2023
  (11.7% of all initial submissions) [S3]. Cumulative acceptance since July
  2016: 5.6% [S4].
- The introduction must "emphasize the incremental contributions of your
  research" with citations "kept to the minimum required to clarify and
  highlight your contributions" [S1]. Overclaimed novelty against the closest
  papers is the standing desk-rejection reason this instruction encodes
  [field norm, unsourced].
- What wins prizes signals what the journal wants: the 2023 awards went to
  papers that either resolved a first-order measurement question (rents and
  intangible capital), stress-tested the field's own evidence base
  (replication crisis; reusing natural experiments), or combined novel data
  with a clean design (bank-lending specialization; household bankruptcy
  moral hazard vs liquidity) [S3].

## b. Methods and identification norms

- Mechanism evidence is expected alongside the headline effect; a credible
  design or an explicitly defended associational stance is the floor
  [field norm, unsourced].
- The Harvey presidential-address standard was delivered from this journal's
  own podium: results built on unreported specification search will "fail to
  hold up," and t-statistics near 2 deserve suspicion, not celebration [S11].
  JF referees are primed by this history [field norm, unsourced].

## c. Data norms

- Post-2023, a data editor verifies that submitted code "cleanly reproduces
  the results in the paper" [S3].
- Full policy in shared-norms §3; JF-specific points: disclosure plan due at
  initial submission; materials due generally within 30 days of conditional
  acceptance; exemptions must be requested at initial submission and granted
  exemptions are noted on the published paper [S2]; proprietary data requires
  an authorization letter from each source [S1].

## d. Writing and presentation norms

- Hard limits: 60 pages total (including internal appendices, references,
  figures, tables) at 1.5 spacing, 12-point font, 1-inch side margins;
  manuscripts exceeding the limits "will be automatically rejected" [S1].
- Abstract: 100 words maximum [S1].
- The paper must be self-contained without the internet appendix [S1].
- The article "should end with a brief nontechnical summary statement of the
  main conclusions" [S1].
- Footnotes at page bottom; displayed equations numbered on the right margin;
  tables and figures embedded or at the end, with all variables clearly
  labeled [S1].

## e. Submission mechanics

- Single-blind refereeing; title page carries authors and abstract [S1].
- Fees: high-income economies $400 (AFA members) / $525 (non-members);
  middle-income $100/$150; low-income free. Refunded on desk rejection or if
  the decision takes more than 100 days (first submission only) [S1].
- Appeals cost a non-refundable $600 [S1].
- A previously rejected manuscript is automatically rejected on resubmission
  [S1].
- R&R resubmission window: 12 months, extensions by editor approval [S3].
- Speed: median turnaround 43 days in 2023; 71.2% of decisions within 70 days;
  8.3% over 100 days [S3].
- Volume context: 1,142 new submissions in 2023, 937 in 2024 [S3, S4]; 72
  articles published in 2023, fewer than JFE or RFS [S3].
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure "for each author by name" placed after the
  title page; submissions without it are returned immediately [S17].
- AI policy: AI may assist drafting, editing, or code; authors must
  fact-check outputs and note the use; AI cannot be a co-author; AI-involved
  analysis requires open-source or time-stamped models with prompt
  documentation [S17].

## Observed norms from exemplars (Phase B)

From a structured reading of 30 recent articles (6 deep reads; see
its `selection_bias_summary` for caveats, including working-paper page
counting):

- Abstract: median 99.5 words; the 100-word cap binds and authors write to it.
- Median 3 authors per paper; median 44 published pages (near the 60-page
  submission ceiling after typesetting).
- Introductions run long by Cochrane's standard: median 9 working-paper pages,
  about 4,600 words, 10 substantive paragraphs. The "three pages is a good
  upper limit" advice describes the ideal, not current JF practice.
- Median 6 main tables, 4.5 figures; every deep-read paper had an internet or
  online appendix.
- 5 of 6 deep-read intros anchor magnitudes with concrete units (percent of
  GDP, dollars, percentage points) in the first pages.
- Numbered hypotheses are rare (1 of 6); JF papers argue in prose and preview
  results as First/Second/Third findings.
- Roadmap paragraphs appear in only a third of intros, consistent with
  Cochrane's advice that they are optional [S10].
- Intro spine across all six: a concrete hook (dated anecdote, quantified
  stylized fact, or analogy), a puzzle or tension, then "In this paper, we..."
  by paragraph 2-3, results preview, contributions, literature placed last.
- Pure theory appears in the sample (Worker Runs, April 2025): JF publishes
  theory when the intro grounds it in an institutional episode with quantified
  stakes.

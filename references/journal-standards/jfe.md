# Journal of Financial Economics (JFE): publication standards

What differs from [shared-norms.md](shared-norms.md). Source tags resolve in
[sources.md](sources.md); untagged judgment calls are marked
`[field norm, unsourced]`.

## a. Desk and novelty criteria

- JFE tolerates a narrower question than JF when the economics is sharp and
  the execution airtight; serious data construction is itself a valued
  contribution [field norm, unsourced].
- No editor report is published, so desk-rejection and R&R rates are not
  publicly stated [sources.md, "not found"]. The refund structure implies desk
  rejection is common and fast: a desk-rejected paper recovers up to $400 of
  the $850 fee, capped at 4 partial refunds per author per calendar year, a
  cap that exists because serial resubmitters exist [S5].
- A paper previously rejected by JFE is desk rejected automatically on
  resubmission with no refund, and the authors are banned from submitting the
  same paper for two years [S5].

## b. Methods and identification norms

- Identification bar comparable to JF; the journal's replication-package
  culture means every number is expected to trace to code the data editor can
  run [S6; field norm for the bar itself, unsourced].
- Era and composition confounds addressed explicitly, equivalence-type
  evidence for null claims, and no column-by-column robustness narration
  [field norm, unsourced].
- Missing closest-paper comparisons are a standing referee complaint;
  positioning against the frontier is expected in the introduction
  [field norm, unsourced].

## c. Data norms

- The strictest codified data-and-code policy of the three:
  - "Data, programs, and details for the computations necessary for
    replication" must be uploaded to Mendeley Data (or a domain repository)
    as soon as possible after acceptance; the link appears on ScienceDirect
    with the article [S6].
  - Disclosed code must cover the full pipeline: programs that transform raw
    data into analysis datasets and programs that generate final outputs [S6].
  - Instructions must state operating system, software versions, all non-core
    modules/libraries/packages, random seeds, and execution order [S6].
  - Restricted data requires a pseudo data set "with enough observations so
    that all of the programs can be run successfully, including any data
    cleaning programs" [S6].
  - A JFE data editor reviews all code for completeness and organization;
    the editor-in-chief "reserves the right to refuse publication" on
    non-compliance [S6].
  - Exemption requests belong in the cover letter of the initial submission
    [S5].
- Recognized exemption categories: proprietary third-party data, confidential
  administrative data, experimental-subject confidentiality, and
  hand-collected data (protected at most two years) [S6].

## d. Writing and presentation norms

- Abstract "not to exceed 100 words" [S5].
- Double-spaced, 12-point or larger font, one-inch margins minimum; no stated
  page cap (unlike JF's 60) [S5].
- Manuscript must be anonymized: no author information or acknowledgments in
  the manuscript PDF; title page uploaded separately [S5].
- Online appendices attach to the end of the main manuscript file at initial
  submission [S5].
- JFE hyphenation is the field's de facto style standard (compound modifiers
  before the noun: after-tax income, risk-free rate; no hyphen after -ly
  adverbs), quoted as authoritative by Cochrane [S10].

## e. Submission mechanics

- Initial submission fee $850; no fee for revisions; partial refund ($400 max)
  on desk rejection; refund if the first decision takes more than 120 days
  [S5].
- Editorial Manager only; required uploads: anonymized manuscript PDF,
  separate title page PDF, combined conflict-of-interest PDF for all authors
  [S5].
- Decision set: desk reject, reject, revise-and-resubmit, conditional accept,
  accept. No reject-and-resubmit [S5].
- R&R resubmission window: 18 months from the decision [S5].
- Volume context: JFE has published more articles per year than JF for many
  years [S3, Figure 3].

## Observed norms from exemplars (Phase B)

From a structured reading of 30 recent articles (6 deep reads; see
its `selection_bias_summary` for caveats):

- Abstract: median 100.5 words; the cap binds.
- Median 3 authors. Published page counts unavailable (article-number
  pagination).
- The shortest introductions of the three journals: median 5 working-paper
  pages, about 2,600 words, 5-6 substantive paragraphs. JFE intros get to the
  design fastest; "In this paper, we..." lands in paragraph 2 in most deep
  reads.
- Median 7.5 main tables and 6 figures: more tables than JF on shorter intros,
  matching the journal's execution-over-framing reputation.
- No deep-read paper used numbered hypotheses; results are narrated
  First/Second in prose.
- 5 of 6 intros anchor magnitudes concretely (a $7.3B loss "four times larger
  than direct losses"; labor share ~50% of GDP).
- Half the intros carry a roadmap paragraph; two-thirds reference an
  online/internet appendix.
- Recurring intro spine: phenomenon or theory-tension opening, the gap named
  explicitly ("little empirical evidence on..."), one clean identification
  event or dataset carried through the whole intro with institutional detail.
